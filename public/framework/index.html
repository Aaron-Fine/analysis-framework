Working framework · Version 0.2 · 2 August 2026

Four questions for a personalized bargaining system.

A phase- and horizon-sensitive method for understanding government without reducing every outcome to one leader’s psychology or inventing a perfectly coherent grand strategy.

Find the fixed goal. Separate the probe from the commitment. Identify who can make it stick. Trace who can make whom pay.
Applicability gate. Begin with the ordinary presidential, partisan, bureaucratic, or strategic baseline. Use a lens only when it changes an ex-ante probability or exposes a falsifier. Mark unused lenses inactive rather than filling every slot.

The four lenses

Each lens has two opposing poles. They are not exclusive categories: a personal decision can become institutionalized, and an implemented action can also send a signal. Record each lens as dominant, supporting, watch, or inactive, then state its current lean.

Ends

Fixed ↔ Fungible

Is this a cause or a deal?

Lean fixed when an objective recurs across time and audiences, survives real cost, aligns personnel and resources, or defines movement identity.

Lean fungible when terms change with the counterpart, concessions or exemptions are exchangeable, private emissaries build bargains, or a visible win matters more than exact terms.

Do not infer doctrine from one speech or transaction merely because someone benefits.

Commitment

Reversible ↔ Committed

Is this a probe or a real commitment?

Lean reversible when a threat changes expectations but spends little money, personnel, authority, or reputation and preserves several off-ramps.

Lean committed when resources are assigned, operational sequencing begins, enforcement repeats, and reversal would impose material or political cost.

A probe is not meaningless. It is strong evidence that a signal occurred today and weak evidence that policy will persist for months.

Authority

Personal ↔ Institutional

Is the leader acting, or the system?

Lean personal when access, loyalty, informal envoys, information control, selective enforcement, or sanction-free reversal change the choice.

Lean institutional when law, budgets, courts, agencies, professional chains, logistics, or inherited routines determine what can be implemented and sustained.

Institutions can restrain, amplify, normalize, or permanently embed a personal directive. Executive action alone is not proof of personalism.

Feedback

Outbound leverage ↔ Inbound exposure

Who can make whom pay?

Lean outbound when a ready instrument can impose fast, visible, reversible, or externally payable costs on an isolated or dependent target.

Lean inbound when adversaries, allies, markets, courts, voters, logistics, or substitution return costs quickly enough to alter the decision.

Name the mechanism. Social harm is not automatically political constraint; ask who bears it, sees it, attributes it, and can act on it.

The commitment ladder

  1. Rhetoric or trial balloon. A signal exists; future options remain open.
  2. Formal direction. An attributable order or announced policy narrows ambiguity.
  3. Resources and personnel. Money, materiel, staff, or legal authority move.
  4. Hard-to-reverse implementation. Reversal now creates material or political loss.
  5. Observable effect. The policy changes behavior or conditions outside the announcing coalition.

Forecast horizons

Every prediction needs a horizon class and an exact resolution date. The horizon changes what is being predicted—not merely confidence.

HorizonForecast objectDefault emphasis
Short · 1–14 daysNext order, strike, pause, appointment, or negotiation stepCommitment and personal authority
Medium · 2–12 weeksBargaining cycle, initial implementation, retaliation, coalition responseEnds, commitment, and feedback
Long · 3–18 monthsPolicy durability, campaign trajectory, accumulated costInstitutional authority and feedback
Horizon · 18 months–5 yearsInstitutionalization, capabilities, coalition change, successionRevealed fixed ends, institutions, cumulative adaptation

Minimum forecast card

  • Resolvable questionOne observable outcome and an exact deadline.
  • Decision unitLeader, small group, agency, legislature, or multiple actors.
  • BaselineWhat the outside view predicts without a Trump-specific explanation.
  • ProbabilityMutually exclusive outcomes rather than “likely” or “unlikely.”
  • Active lensesOnly lenses that materially change the forecast.
  • CounterpartyIts capability, red line, internal politics, and likely response.
  • CruxAn observable that would move probability by at least ten points.
  • ResolutionSource, yes/no criteria, update history, and eventual score.

Mandatory audits

Evidence quality, cost incidence, perspective, legality, and ethics are required checks—not extra causal lenses. Every application should ask who benefits, who bears human and material costs, whose agency is missing, and whether descriptive symmetry is hiding moral asymmetry.

Where the old model went

  • Doctrine becomes the fixed side of Ends.
  • Interests become the fungible side of Ends and channels within Feedback.
  • Whim is retired as an unexplained residual; Commitment and Authority provide observable replacements.
  • Personalism becomes the personal side of Authority.
  • Patrimonialism becomes a diagnostic conclusion after repeated evidence, not an assumed operating system.
  • Operational affordability becomes outbound leverage; incidence and external response become inbound exposure.
  • The meal fights back names the feedback principle: every actor and coupled system gets another move.

Failure modes

  • Hindsight fit: require a preregistered probability delta and falsifier.
  • Trump exceptionalism: begin with an ordinary-presidency or institutional baseline.
  • Universal bluff: concrete execution evidence overrides a prior for bargaining theater.
  • Leader-only analysis: identify counterpart agency and exogenous shocks.
  • Slot filling: if a lens does not change the forecast, mark it inactive.
Version 0.2 is a synthesis with a public scorecard, not a claim to a new general theory. Its value depends on whether the prediction ledger produces discriminating forecasts and preserves failures.
