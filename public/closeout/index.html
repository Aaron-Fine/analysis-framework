Experiment closed · 6 November 2026 · Framework 0.3 final

Closeout assessment

A useful way to read a situation. Not a forecasting edge.

The ledger did not show that the four lenses improve prediction. It did show where they make an explanation sharper than the ordinary story, and where they add nothing. This page records both, so the method can be reused for what it is good at.

Bottom line. Two scored forecasts, both hits, and both on outcomes in which the existing condition continued. One recorded miss on a discontinuity. Four forecasts were withdrawn unscored, and one of them was heading toward a miss on its leading branch. No matched baseline was ever recorded, so the record cannot separate framework skill from an ordinary status-quo forecast. The framework's demonstrated value is explanatory and procedural.

Why it was not predictive

  • The lenses explain friction better than breaks. Commitment, authority, and feedback are good at saying why things are slow, partial, or stuck. Both hits were persistence outcomes. Both clear failures were transitions: July's escalation, and a framework-level Greenland deal arriving faster than the leading branch allowed. A method that is strongest on inertia will look good whenever nothing changes, and that tells you little.
  • No baseline, no test. Parsimony and matched baselines entered in version 0.3, after every live forecast had opened. Without a recorded status-quo or reference-class distribution, a hit cannot be credited to the lenses. The rule against reconstructing baselines afterward was correct, and it leaves the question permanently open for this ledger.
  • Lenses did not map to probabilities. Each lens read was qualitative. The numbers were judgment informed by the reads, with no stated rule for how much a lens should move a probability. That makes the framework hard to falsify as a forecaster.
  • Measurement gaps blocked resolution. Two of six questions, tariffs and the administrative state, could not be scored on their own terms. The gaps were found before closure and correctly left unrepaired, but a question that cannot resolve cannot test anything.
  • The sample was too small and too correlated. Six forecasts, three of them linked to one conflict or one negotiation, on horizons that often outran the experiment. Calibration could never have been shown at this scale.
  • Interpretation and forecast came apart. Greenland is the clearest case. The framework read the situation correctly (negotiated access, Greenlandic consent indispensable, sovereignty untouched) and still placed most probability on delay. Getting the mechanism right did not get the timing right.

Where it adds explanatory power

Against an ordinary explanation (a president pursuing stated goals through normal institutions, or allies bargaining in the usual way), each lens earned its place in some cases and not in others. The test used here is the framework's own: did the lens change the explanation, or supply a distinction the ordinary account missed?

Commitment: separating the signal from the build

This was the most consistently useful lens. The ordinary account tends to treat a signed or announced policy as the policy. The commitment checklist (announced, authorized, funded, implemented, sustained) kept the Greenland posture question honest. The agreement names two new sites, but at closure there was only a survey, a planning cycle, and no contract. On tariffs, it explained why losing one legal authority did not end the program: collection never stopped, and replacement statutes were already in hand. Use it when there is a gap between what has been said or signed and what has been built, paid for, or enforced.

Authority: finding who can make it stick

Useful wherever outcomes run through consent points the leader does not control. In Greenland, the path ran exactly through the actors the lens named: Greenlandic consent, Danish treaty authority, then two parliaments and a diplomatic note. Personal pressure set the agenda; institutions set the form. On the administrative state, the lens explained how a personal directive becomes durable machinery through an OPM rule, an executive order, and a Supreme Court ruling. Use it when an outcome needs several authorizations, or when the question is whether personal control will outlast the person.

Feedback: why coercion does not equal concession

In Iran, the ordinary account expected pressure to produce a deal. The feedback lens held that the ability to inflict cost is not the ability to secure terms when the other side can return costs. That explained months of attrition better than either settlement story. On tariffs, it correctly predicted that courts, markets, and counterparts would reshape exemptions and deals (the China reduction, the Canada list revisions) rather than the direction. Use it when both sides can impose costs, and you need to say who bears them, who sees them, and who can act on them.

Ends: fixed or fungible

The weakest lens on its own. Its one clear contribution was on tariffs: treating the direction as fixed and the legal basis, rates, and exemptions as tradeable described the post-court rebuild accurately. Elsewhere, inferred priorities added little beyond what the ordinary account already assumed. Use it when stated justifications keep changing while the action stays the same.

The discipline mattered more than the lenses

The most durable gains came from the ledger rules, not the lenses: exact resolution criteria, preserved misses, the split between thesis confirmation and scored resolution, and the refusal to repair rules after the fact. They stopped Greenland's signing from being booked as a forecasting win. They also exposed the tariff and administrative-state measurement gaps instead of hiding them. Those habits transfer to any analysis, with or without the four questions.

How to use it after the experiment

  1. Write the ordinary explanation first, and say what it predicts.
  2. Run the four questions as a checklist for what the ordinary explanation might be missing. Most cases will activate one or two lenses.
  3. Turn each active lens into an observable milestone: a vote, an award, a transit count, a ruling. Do not turn it into a probability weight.
  4. Keep the interpretation separate from any forecast. A right reading of the mechanism does not license confidence about timing.
  5. If you do forecast, record the baseline distribution beside your own, define every measure before the outcome, and expect persistence calls to score well for reasons that have nothing to do with the method.

What a real predictive test would have needed

Dozens of questions rather than six. Mostly short horizons that resolve inside the experiment. A recorded baseline distribution for every question, published at opening. A stated, preregistered direction and size for each lens's adjustment. Questions chosen to include transitions rather than only continuing conditions. And, as the research page proposed, a second analyst coding the same evidence blind. None of that is planned. The ledger closes as an honest small sample, not as evidence for or against a new theory.

Loose ends left visible

  • Tariffs: the Court of International Trade ruling on the Section 301 forced-labor action. An unstayed loss would move coverage sharply down.
